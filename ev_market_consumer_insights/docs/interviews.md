# Interview notes

*Three short, informal conversations in Metro Detroit, October 2026, held in person by Sam Hakim.
Everyone agreed out loud to take part, stayed anonymous, and could skip any question. These notes
record only what people said. Where a topic wasn't covered, I left it blank instead of filling it
in later from memory.*

## Who I talked to

| | Who | When |
|---|---|---|
| Person 1 | A small-business owner | October 2026 |
| Person 2 | An electric car owner who lives in a condominium | October 2026 |
| Person 3 | Someone who recently bought a new gas pickup truck from a dealer | October 2026 |

These are people I could reach easily. They aren't a random or representative group, so their views
were used only to decide what to look for in the data, never to say how common a view is.

I wrote down the concerns of persons 1 and 2 together, so below they're reported as one set.

## Persons 1 and 2

**Charging: where and what it costs**
- Charging isn't ready yet. Even where public chargers exist, they're expensive. Their rough guess
  was about $30 to $40 for a public charge against about $10 to $15 at home. (These are their
  estimates, not measured prices.)
- Not everyone can put in a home charger, especially people in small or shared homes like condos
  and apartments.

**Repairs**
- People worry about electrical problems in any car, gas or electric.
- Electrical repairs feel too complicated and too expensive.
- Very few mechanics know how to handle them, so owners end up at the dealer. Most people can't
  afford that and don't want it. They'd rather use their local, cheaper mechanic.

**Winter**
- Cold weather hurts the battery. Very cold days drain it and slow down charging, especially in
  snowy Michigan: "who wants to deal with that?"

**Other views**
- One view raised was that the oil and fuel industry still has a lot of influence and wants gas
  stations to keep doing well. This is a personal opinion. In the study I only looked at actual
  state policies, like special fees for electric cars, not at anyone's motives.

**Not covered:** their current cars and how they chose them, what would change their next purchase,
and where they get their information.

## Person 3: recent pickup buyer who didn't choose an electric car

**What they bought**
- A new gas pickup truck from a dealer. Not a hybrid, not electric.

**Why not electric or hybrid**
- Asked whether they had considered an electric car or a hybrid, they said the main reasons against
  were how hard it is to refuel and the short range, meaning how far the car goes on one charge.

**Charging**
- Chargers aren't everywhere yet, the way gas stations are.

**What would change their mind**
- They think electric cars are getting more popular as batteries get better. Once chargers are as
  common as gas stations, getting one would be "much better." So this was "not yet", not "never."

**Not covered:** repairs, winter, and where they get their information.

A follow-up question for future truck and SUV buyers: did towing or long trips play a part?
